## Design Context

### Users
Fellow engineers and architects — peers who arrive to read seriously. They're technically sophisticated, pattern-match quickly on credibility signals, and have low tolerance for marketing fluff. They're here to learn, not to be impressed. Some are also considering Praveen for advisory or senior roles; they read the writing to evaluate thinking quality, not just resume credentials.

### Brand Personality
Approachable and generous — like a senior colleague who genuinely loves to teach. Warm but not effusive. Clear but not dumbed down. The voice should feel like the person in the room who always has a useful framing and takes time to explain it well. Three words: **generous, rigorous, direct**.

### Aesthetic Direction
Editorial-style, publishing-first. The existing design (Charter serif body, restrained color palette, wide content column) reflects this well. The interface should disappear and let the content lead. Both light and dark themes are supported.

Anti-reference: anything that feels like a SaaS marketing site — gradient CTAs, "Join 10,000+ subscribers" social proof banners, vague mission statements, hero metrics.

### Design Principles
1. **Content earns the chrome** — navigation, gating, and UI frames should be minimal; never louder than the writing itself.
2. **Speak as Praveen, not as the site** — copy should sound like a person talking to a peer, not a product talking to a user.
3. **Respect the reader's intelligence** — don't over-explain, don't hedge, don't add filler words for friendliness.
4. **Every gate should feel like an invitation, not a wall** — subscription prompts and tutorial locks should give a reason, not demand compliance.
5. **Labels should tell you what's actually there** — navigation and UI labels should be specific and honest, not aspirational or generic.
