## Written Script (as Praveen)

### Opening

> I teach because peers matter. You didn't get here by accident—you're curious about AI systems, and that matters. So let's be direct: the AI ecosystem is vast, and most tutorials will sell you a story. Ours won't.

> I built three tutorial paths on this site. They're not a funnel to "buy my course." They're three honest answers to three questions you might be asking right now.

### Three Paths

**Path 1: "I'm new to AI. Where do I even start?"**

Start with **Agentic AI in the SDLC**. Not "what is machine learning." Not "GPT-4 explained." But: what does it mean when an AI system *acts* instead of just *responds*? How does that change governance, accountability, risk? This is the conceptual foundation everything else builds on.

Then go deeper with **LLM Mastery** — understand what LLMs actually do, how they reason, where they break. Not flashy. Not hype. Honest.

> **Why here first?** Because you can't make good decisions about AI systems if you don't understand what autonomy changes. Spending weeks on neural networks when you should be learning agent architecture is a waste of your time.

**Path 2: "I want the GitHub Certified: Agentic AI Developer credential (GH-600)."**

I wrote the tutorial. It's designed for one goal: pass on the first attempt, with real understanding, not memorized answers. 

It covers all six exam domains, weighted by the actual exam. It shows you the anti-patterns that trip up strong engineers (Domain 6 governance, Domain 4 evaluation—the places reasoning leaks). And it includes hands-on work requirements, because reading doesn't equal doing.

> **Why this path?** Certifications are valuable when they test *judgment*, not trivia. GH-600 does. This tutorial teaches the judgment.

**Path 3: "I want to understand how to *build* AI systems in production."**

Go to **System Design for AI** and **LangGraph Temporal Workflows**. These are for people who've learned the fundamentals and now want to see how real systems decompose, orchestrate, handle failure, track state.

> **Why here last?** Because you can't architect what you don't understand. Learn the concepts first. Then learn the patterns. Then learn the code.

### How to Choose

- **Honest about your background?** Pick the path that matches where you are, not where you wish you were.
- **Short on time?** Take GH-600. It's tight, focused, first-attempt pass.
- **Want the full picture?** Start with fundamentals, then go deep with system design.
- **Already in AI?** Jump to system design or certification. You can fill gaps as you go.

### What You Won't Get Here

- Hand-waving about "AGI" or "transformative AI."
- Hype about what AI can do (you already know the hype).
- Motivational speeches instead of rigor.
- Tutorials written for someone else's attention span.

### What You Will Get

- Honesty about what's actually hard.
- Reasoning, not trivia. If I'm telling you something, you'll understand *why*.
- Respect for your time. No filler. No fluff.
- A clear path to competence.

### The Offer

Pick a path. Work through it. Do the hands-on exercises (yes, they're required—reading alone doesn't cut it). If at the end you don't feel more capable, I haven't done my job.

> You came here because you wanted to learn seriously. Let's make that count.

## Key Tone Notes for Adaptation

**If using this as copy on the site:**
- Direct, peer-to-peer (not sales)
- Acknowledge the learner's intelligence
- Be specific about prerequisites and scope
- Flag what's *not* here (realistic scope setting)
- Emphasize hands-on work as non-negotiable
- Respect time as a scarce resource

**If adapting for video/illustration:**
- Praveen as the constant figure (not changing per path)
- Three paths branching equally
- Visual metaphor: guide with options, not gatekeeper with one door
- Avoid marketing angles ("Join 10,000+ learners," social proof badges, etc.)
- Show rigor visually: complexity, choice, depth

**Brand voice consistency:**
- Generous (clear, helpful, no gatekeeping)
- Rigorous (exact, no hand-waving, built on ground truth)
- Direct (no filler, speaks to peer intelligence)
